comfyatmos.ini

The settings file of the test client, as the tests ran with it. A test's own Atmosphere controls (its test settings) are applied over the matching values here.

[fog]

The fog: thick at the ground and thinning upward. It darkens what lies behind it and is lit by the sun and by the sky. Needs [depth] enabled. With [volume] enabled it is drawn with the volumetric light, and shows shafts where things shade the sun. With [volume] enabled = 0 it draws alone, with no shadow map: the sun lights all of it, as at Volumetric Light Strength 25. The game's own fog stays, as the far wall.

density0.0025fog per yard at the ground. 0.0025 lets through 60% of what is 200 yards away

reach200yards: how far a line of sight gathers fog. It fades out over the last 40%; past it the game's own fog is the far wall
skyDistance75yards: the same for a line of sight to the sky (0 = the sky gets no fog)

[depth]

A depth buffer the shaders can read (the INTZ format) swapped in for the client's. The volumetric light reads it. Off by default.

enabled1

seeThroughNearMounted15yards: the same while you ride a mount. The game fades the larger model from farther off, and the far side of the horse showed through
waterDepth11 = the water writes depth, so the sun shadows and the light see its surface; 0 = as the game draws it (they see what lies under the water)

[volume]

Volumetric light: the fog glowing where the sun reaches it and dark where leaves and walls shade it. The lamp-in-a-foggy-room look, fixed in the world as you move the camera. Needs [depth] and [shadow] enabled. Alt+F11 toggles it.

enabled1

steps64samples along each line of sight (more holds up over a long maxDistance)
maxDistance190yards along each line of sight (keep at or under [shadow] range)
anisotropy0.0700 = glows the same from every side; toward 1 = mostly when looking toward the sun
bias0.5yards of slack in the shadow test, against speckle on sunlit surfaces
leafShade1how much of the sun the leaves stop in the air, 0..1. 1 = the light comes through the gaps between the leaves only, in shafts. The ground's own is [sunshadows] leafShade
color0xFFE6BERGB of the light (default: warm late-morning)

debug01 = show the glow alone, white, to see its shape. For finding faults, one stage of the march each: 2 = white if it runs at all, 3 = the depth it reads, 4 = distance marched (white = maxDistance), 5 = share of each ray in sunlight
occlusion11 = the light fades when the sun's disc is behind terrain on screen, as for the rays ([rays] occlusionRadius and occlusionFull). The shadow map reaches only [shadow] depth, so without this a sun setting behind the far horizon lights the fog

[sunshadows]

Sun shadows: trees, buildings and characters shade the ground and walls. They come from the volumetric light's shadow map, so they draw only while [volume] draws. They follow the sun's height and [night] strength. Within [shadow] nearRange yards of you they use a finer map (0.016 yards a texel at size 4096), further out the far one (0.12). F12 logs the draw (lines starting "sunshadows:").

lodStart80yards from the camera where lodBias starts

bias3texels of slack in the depth test at the least; more as the sun gets lower, so flat ground does not shade itself in stripes. In texels of each map. Higher = no bands where a surface shades itself, but shadows start further from what casts them
minGap0yards: a blocker nearer the surface than this, along the sun, does not shade it. Against a model or a tree shading itself; shadows then start this far from where something touches the ground

[lamps]

Lamps, lanterns and torches: the fog glows around them, and lampposts light the ground and walls near them as the game's torches do. The lights are the game's own (torches and braziers, also those NPCs carry) and lampposts, found by their glow sprite. It uses the volumetric light's depth and camera, so it draws only while [volume] draws, but it does not follow the sun and draws at night too. F12 logs the lights it draws (lines starting "lampglow:").

[rays]

Sun rays: bright sky near the sun streaks outward, through gaps in the trees and clouds. Drawn after the world and before the UI, so the UI gets none. Cheap, and needs neither [depth] nor [shadow]. The rays follow the sun in [sun]. Ctrl+F11 toggles them.

enabled1
strength40
maxExposure5.7
relThreshold0.200..1, a pixel casts rays if it is this bright relative to the brightest thing on screen, so sky gaps in a dim forest still cast. Lower = more things glow
threshold0.200..1, absolute minimum brightness, so dark scenes do not streak from dim lights
radius0.8how far from the sun, in screen heights, pixels still cast rays. Keep this tight: wider lets bright clouds elsewhere in the sky cast rays too
falloff2.0how sharply that fades with distance: 1 = straight line, 2+ = hugs the sun
length0.850..1, ray length as a fraction of the way from each pixel toward the sun
maxLength0.6...but never longer than this many screen heights (matters when the sun is far off screen)
maxAngle60degrees between where you look and the sun at which rays are gone. Over 90, rays stream in from the top of the screen with the sun out of view, fed by whatever sky is there, so the view brightened and darkened as the camera tilted. The shafts from a sun out of view come from the volumetric light instead
viewFalloff1.0how quickly rays weaken as you turn from the sun toward maxAngle; higher = faster
parallel0.00 = rays fan out from the sun (how real sunbeams look in perspective); 1 = parallel streaks all falling the same way: a stylised look, not steadier
adaptTime0.5seconds for "what counts as bright" to follow the scene; 0 = instant (flickers)
coverTime0.15seconds the rays and the volumetric light take to fade when something covers the sun, and to come back when it is clear
decay0.960.5..1, falloff along each ray; lower = shorter, softer shafts
soften1pixels (of the mask, which is 1/downscale of the screen) the mask is blurred before the rays are drawn, so small gaps between leaves do not make rays jump. 0 = none
smooth00..0.95, how much of the last frame is kept, moved with the sun. Leaf edges smaller than a pixel flicker as the camera moves; this keeps the rays steady. 0 = none
color0xFFE6BERGB of the light (default: warm late-morning)
passes31..3 blur passes; fewer is cheaper but shows banding
downscale2work at 1/N resolution per axis
debugView01 = show only the bright-pixel mask, 2 = show only the rays, 3 = show the sky kept before the clouds (for skyOnly). For tuning
placement00 = between world and UI; 1 = over everything, UI included (fallback)
mask11 = a pixel casts where it shows sky, from the depth buffer: steady as the camera moves. 0 = by brightness, relative to the brightest thing on screen (relThreshold)
skyOnly11 = only the sky casts, not the clouds: lit clouds near the sun glow otherwise. The kept sky has no glow, so it is dimmer; relThreshold 0.20 suits it. 0 = all
secondMoon1at night the volumetric light follows the larger of the two moons. 1 = the other moon casts rays too; 0 = rays from the larger moon only
occlusion11 = rays fade when the sun itself is covered, by a mountain or a wall. Tested by depth when [depth] is on, else by brightness (needs skyOnly; heavy fog defeats it). 0 = the sky above a ridge casts rays over it with the sun behind it
occlusionRadius0.08screen heights around the sun that are tested

[night]

Sun rays and volumetric light at night. At night they follow the moons, which are high in the sky, so without this the rays and the light at night are as strong as by day. Night is told from the game clock ([client] clockAddr), so it follows the time comfytime sets. F12 logs the game time and the value in use.

strength25the dial, 0..100: the rays and the light at night, as % of their day strength. 100 = as by day, 0 = none at night
dusk20hour the change to night starts (fractions allowed: 20.5 = 20:30)
dawn5hour the change to day starts
fade1.5hours each change takes: full night from dusk + fade to dawn, full day from dawn + fade to dusk

tint0.650..1: how far the night leans toward moonColor (Moonlight Colour)

[bench]

The benchmark. Alt+F12 runs four steps: rays + volumetric light + fog, rays + the fog alone, rays, nothing. Each step runs for settle seconds, then is measured for measure seconds. Then the settings go back as they were, and a table goes to comfyfog.log: frame rate, the slowest 1% of frames, and the GPU and CPU time of our own passes, with the light split into its parts. Play with the light on for a minute first, so its cache is full. Stand still, do not move the mouse, and face the sun. F11 stops it.

settle5seconds before each step is measured (shaders compile, the shadow cache fills)
measure5seconds each step is measured
